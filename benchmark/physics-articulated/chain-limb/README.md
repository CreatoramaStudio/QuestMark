# chain-limb: 12-link chain and limb chains, Jolt vs PhysX

Part of [physics-articulated](../README.md) (versions, build, skeleton file, size labels, metric definitions and the status of the results are described there; read its "Status of the results" first). Results come from the original runs; the sources were only renamed for publication.

## Question

Can a 12-link chain with link masses from 50 kg to 50 t (1000:1 end to end) be simulated at a fixed 120 Hz and 200 Hz with Jolt used directly and with PhysX CPU reduced-coordinate articulations, and what happens on limb chains derived from a humanoid skeleton? Thresholds are harness choices (see the parent README).

## Scenario (identical in both libraries)

World +z up, gravity 9.81 m/s^2 along -z. Twelve box links along +x from a pivot at the origin, link `i` spanning x = 2i to 2i+2 m. The hinge axis is world z for all 12 joints (`--axis y` switches to a perpendicular axis); the first hinge joins link 0 to the world. Limits +-2 rad, no drive, no joint friction, zero body damping. Links never collide with each other. Box half extents (1 m, h, h) with `h = 0.5 * cbrt(m / m_max)`. Launch: rigid rotation about the pivot so the tip end moves at 30 m/s into a 5 cm thick static wall (front face y = 2.4 m). Friction 0.5, restitution 0. Every 2.0 s of simulated time the world is rebuilt from scratch (an episode); step times are measured around the physics update call only.

Mass profiles (`--profile`): `geometric` (root 50 t to tip 50 kg, neighbour ratio about 1.88) and `alternating` (50 t and 50 kg links alternate, so every joint joins masses 1000:1 apart; an extra stress case).

Backends: `backend_jolt.cpp` (Jolt 5.7.0, bodies + `HingeConstraint`, default iterations 10 velocity / 2 position unless `--iters`, `LinearCast` motion quality when CCD is on, sleeping off, a pair filter that blocks link-link contacts) and `backend_physx.cpp` (PhysX 5.13.0 CPU, articulation with a fixed base and 12 revolute joints, default iterations as printed by the backend string; link CCD flag plus scene CCD flag when CCD is on; collision groups block link-link contacts). `--backend physx-maximal` is a control: 12 `PxRigidDynamic` + `PxRevoluteJoint` with the same masses, frames, limits and gravity (PGS solver; `--physx-tgs on` for TGS).

Limb scenario (`--scenario limb --limb leg|arm --size_label <label> --limb-case hold|swing|wall|impact --fbx <skeleton.fbx>`): `limb.cpp` reads the skeleton (bone names in the parent README) with ufbx, builds four bodies and four hinges per limb (a small gimbal body with abduction and pitch hinges, then two or three link boxes), masses by bone-bounding-box volume share of the size's weight, PD drives to angle 0 (`kp = I_eff (2 pi f)^2`, `kd = 2 zeta sqrt(kp I_eff)`, f = 2 Hz, zeta = 1, torque cap = max(5 x static gravity torque, 0.25 x sum m g r_perp); all assumptions). Cases: `hold` 4 s (all hinges PD-held, root fixed, no ground), `swing` 8 s (pitch hinge started at -0.9 rad, gravity perpendicular to the hinge axes), `wall` 2 s (rigid launch at 30 m/s into a 5 cm wall placed 80 ms ahead of the leading box corner), `impact` 2 s (hold plus a 2000 kg 1 m cube at an assumed 30 m/s aimed at the centre of the last link). `--worst-axes on` forces every hinge axis parallel to gravity (leg standing, arm in the horizontal pose). `--dump-skeleton <fbx>` prints every bone.

Reading (proved for these cases only: alternating 50 t / 50 kg, 12 links, float32, the two library versions above, 120 and 200 Hz, default iterations unless marked, one machine). (1) With gravity parallel to the hinge axis, Jolt's anchor error exceeds 0.01 m by step 8 to 19 in every variant and goes non-finite in 10 of the 16 z cases; it does not need the impact. (2) None of the single remedies cured it at 120 Hz (inertia multipliers made the error larger); the thicker light box (same mass) at 200 Hz kept the error at 0.357 m with no 1 m exceedance, but not at 120 Hz. (3) With the hinge axis perpendicular to gravity Jolt did not blow up in any variant. (4) The PhysX maximal-coordinate chain with the same masses also loses closure (above 1 m at step 135 and 344), so the contrast is reduced-coordinate articulation against maximal-coordinate joints; whether Jolt's solver or its default iteration counts (10/2 against PhysX's 4/1) cause the larger Jolt error was not separated. Not checked (hypotheses): why the thicker box helps at 200 Hz (the long-axis inertia ratio of 100000:1 is one candidate); why inertia multipliers made it worse; whether another Jolt constraint type, soft constraint settings or raised iterations in combination with the remedies cure the z case.

Reading: the Jolt PD hold sags more than PhysX for the same nominal gains (hold, 120 Hz: PD angle error 0.024 rad leg and 0.096 rad arm in Jolt against 0.0057 and 0.0012 rad in PhysX; the cause was not isolated). The worst-axis hold is not the 1000:1 chain: its neighbour mass ratios are 10:1 to 3:1 (gimbal 10:1), so it does not repeat the chain result; Jolt's hinge axis error there (0.312 rad arm at 120 Hz) is the weakest number of the matrix. The largest link speeds agree between the libraries (for example leg swing 26.6 m/s in both), which supports that both ran the same physical case; wall penetration is similar for the arm and about twice as large in Jolt for the leg.

## Corrections found while building (harness bugs, fixed before the published runs)

- Jolt layer filters must be symmetric: with a one-sided filter the impact cube passed through the limb in Jolt (speed unchanged at 30 m/s) while PhysX stopped it; fixed with a separate broad-phase layer for the cube.
- The impact is aimed at the centre of the last link (aiming at the tip made the cube graze the lower box edge).

## Limits

Chain: one 12-link chain against one thin wall; limb: bounding boxes, assumed joint limits and PD gains, left leg and left arm of one skeleton, torso not simulated, weights read as metric tons (short tons only for h85m), no `physx-maximal` or Jolt remedies on the limbs, PhysX speculative CCD not tried separately, one configuration of each library (default tolerances; PhysX default tolerance scale). The follow-up energy columns are not valid (the launch energy is tiny at tip speed 0.1 m/s; with the inertia multiplier the energy formula uses the unmultiplied inertia). The 0.01 m and 1 m levels are arbitrary harness thresholds.
